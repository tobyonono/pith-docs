Pith's design principles are the lived logic that guides every decision across every engagement — from a quick landing page to a long-running product build. This page sets out the four core pillars and explains precisely how they should show up in day-to-day design work.

## The Four Pillars

These four principles aren't a values statement for a pitch deck — they're the lived logic behind every decision we make at Pith. They apply equally to a homepage redesign, a design system audit, a quick-turnaround landing page, or a multi-month product build. When a project feels off-track, it's usually because one of these has been compromised.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Taste > Tech" icon="palette">
    Aesthetic excellence can only be cultivated through attention, intention, and genuine cultural engagement. The tools we use are interchangeable; the sensibility we bring is not. Anyone can generate output — a template, a prompt, a theme — but cultivating real taste requires sustained exposure to great work across disciplines, a willingness to be changed by it, and the rigour to apply those lessons with precision. We hold our aesthetic judgement to a high standard because the people we build for deserve nothing less.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Conviction Creates" icon="bolt">
    Authentic creativity comes from deep, considered beliefs — not borrowed inspiration or trend-chasing. Every design decision should have a *why* behind it: a reason rooted in the brief, the brand, the audience, or the cultural moment. Conviction doesn't mean rigidity. It means being able to articulate and defend your choices, and being willing to change them when a better argument emerges. Work made from genuine conviction is felt by the people who experience it, even when they can't say exactly why.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Wonder Works" icon="magnifying-glass">
    The best solutions come from questioning assumptions. Approach every brief with genuine curiosity — about the problem, the client's world, the audience's actual behaviour, and the edges of what's technically possible. Don't accept the first frame you're handed. Ask why things are the way they are before deciding whether to change them. Curiosity is what transforms a competent execution into a genuinely interesting one. It's also what makes the work sustainable: studios that stop being curious stop producing anything worth looking at.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Build Together" icon="users">
    Co-creation consistently leads to better outcomes than lone-genius execution. The most successful projects at Pith are the ones where clients, collaborators, and studio members are genuinely building together — sharing work-in-progress early, surfacing tensions honestly, and treating feedback as a resource rather than a threat. Be transparent about your thinking. Be flexible when the brief evolves. Be responsive when partners need you. The relationship is the work, and the work is the relationship.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Applying the Principles

Principles mean nothing if they only live in documentation. Here's how the four pillars should actively show up in day-to-day design work at Pith.

### In Figma Reviews

Before presenting any frame — internally or to a client — ask yourself whether every decision has a *why* behind it (**Conviction Creates**). You shouldn't be explaining a layout you aren't sure about; you should be presenting a position. At the same time, leave genuine space for **Build Together**: share explorations, not just final directions, and frame reviews as conversations rather than reveals. If you're only showing the polished end-state, you're optimising for approval rather than the best outcome.

### In Client Presentations

Lead with cultural and strategic context before you open the Figma file (**Taste > Tech**, **Wonder Works**). Help clients understand the references, the reasoning, and the problem framing before they see the solution. This raises the quality of feedback dramatically — clients who understand *why* you made a choice can engage with it meaningfully, rather than reacting to surface aesthetics. Invite questions about the assumptions you've made. If a client surfaces something you haven't considered, that's the principle of **Wonder Works** doing its job.

### In Design Decisions

When you're stuck between two directions, don't default to the safer option or the one that's easier to execute. Default to the one that's true to the brief and the brand — even if it's harder to sell (**Conviction Creates**). If neither option feels right, that's usually a signal to go back a step: more research, more reference-gathering, a conversation with a collaborator (**Wonder Works**, **Build Together**). Taste isn't about having an immediate instinct; it's about knowing when to slow down and look harder (**Taste > Tech**).

<Note>
  These principles apply equally when you're the most senior person in the room and when you're the most junior. Conviction isn't hierarchy-dependent, and curiosity doesn't stop being valuable once you're experienced. Hold yourself to these regardless of the project size or the client budget.
</Note>
